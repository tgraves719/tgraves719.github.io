Concept mock-up of the visor mount on the headset with the ZED camera on front. Used to position the text on the mount for printing.
Hard to believe this was 3D printed! Using HP Multi Jet Fusion 3D-printing technology, I was able to print this camera mount with nylon and print it in color to display our logo!

I will spend some time calibrating the camera with the VR headset, but will be able to record more tests with more instruments and get the final pieces in motion for the first public performance. The recital date will be announced this upcoming week!

Special thanks to Resin Foundry for making the part quickly and with some crazy cool technology. If anyone in the Springs needs to 3D print with SLA or MJF tech, hit them up! Also, If anyone needs normal stuff 3D printed with FDM, I recommend Catz Design Farm who gave me the referral to Resin Foundry when they couldn't print this part with traditional 3D printing.

Very excited for the future of Mixed Reality. Our current Beta prototype uses a PC-VR headset with a 3D printed mount and a depth-camera mounted to the front. This solution is expensive and not very effective to scale with multiple audience members. There are standalone devices like the HoloLens 2 but are more expensive. Then comes in this beautiful piece of tech, the Lynx R-1. This standalone VR/AR headset has built-in cameras for AR passthrough and runs on the same chipset as the Quest 2. There will be a kickstarter for the headset in September and we will absolutely get one for Portraits. At just a few hundred dollars, this option is vastly cheaper and much higher fidelity (both resolution, framerate and FOV) than our beta prototype. My recital will still use the beta hardware, but future performances in 2022 and beyond will feature more MR participants and better visuals with a device like this!

The past year has been very challenging on all of us. I honestly have been struggling. Struggling to work on a project for performing arts without any performances, struggling to stay motivated through a pandemic. I am sure many of you can relate. I still wanted to continue work with VR/AR and music so I shifted gears, and started working on an educational tool to help teach basic conducting in a college classroom. I recently had the opportunity to bring my VR/AR rig down to CSU-P and got to do what I love, sharing the gifts of music and mixed reality with my peers and mentors. I demonstrated a rudimentary conducting VR app, but also got the chance to showcase some of my progress in Portraits of Change. With the pandemic soon recessing as we ramp up vaccinations, the prospect of live performance with live audiences become possible again. There will be a Portraits performance as my senior recital by the end of the year. I look forward to announcing details soon, and collaborating with more musicians to bring our music to light! Here is a short demonstration of the Beta prototype and software with the man who taught me piano, Greg Creager!

On March 15, 2019 Portraits of Change became a fiscally sponsored project. Almost exactly a year ago, we launched our online presence and have since made tremendous progress in Mixed Reality technology along with its artistic application within music. We have shared our progress with the community at live events and through online videos. I am proud to announce today- March 16th, 2020 that Portraits of Change has entered it's Beta! Using the Valve Index, ZED Mini stereoscopic depth camera, LEAP Motion IR sensor, streamlined MIDI interface and the Beta PoC Software has allowed us to create true augmented reality using pass-through VR. Even when we pick a new AR HMD platform for 1.0, we will still use this tracking system on stage to give us greater control of the mixed reality live performance. Expect public demonstrations once the Coronavirus isn't a public health crisis, but feel free to reach out to me if you would like to try out our Beta experience! It will be available for testing Q2 2020.
