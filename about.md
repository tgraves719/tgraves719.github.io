      <div class="space-y-4 text-lg text-gray-300">
        <p>I'm a musician and developer working on what happens when a human practice is made machine-computable, and whom the resulting system then serves. In practice that means three things at once: an open-source contract assistant piloting with a UFCW local, three published years of the imperial print register of British Punjab, and a set of instruments that put live music into mixed reality.</p>
        <p class="text-gray-400">Classically trained, electronically adopted, a jazz lover at heart. I produce, play trumpet and sing, and I hold a BA in Music from CSU Pueblo. I've worked in education, fabrication, machining, IT and frontline retail — which is where I learned how reliably technical complexity excludes people, and who benefits when it does.</p>
        <p class="text-gray-400">I went on strike with my union at Safeway in 2025 and played music on the picket line. Everything on the research side of this site follows from that: a workplace is an information environment, contract literacy is information literacy with material consequences, and the side without the tool is the side worth building for.</p>
        <p class="text-gray-400">The theoretical half of the work sits between critical theory and analytical social science — Adorno and Habermas read against the problems that computational modelling of social life actually presents today. The test I keep returning to is contestability: when a system decides something about you, can you raise an objection that somebody is obliged to answer in reasons you can dispute?</p>
        <p class="text-gray-400">Hit me up if you want to collaborate, duel, barter, or talk about the existential enigma that is our times.</p>
